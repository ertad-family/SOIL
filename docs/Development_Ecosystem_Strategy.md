## 1. Executive Summary

This document outlines SOIL's development ecosystem strategy, designed to enable global scaling through community-driven development while maintaining centralized control over data and platform integrity.

### 1.1 Core Strategic Model

SOIL adopts a "GitLab-style" approach: open source code with centralized deployment. This model maximizes community contribution while protecting the platform's core asset — the organizational mortality dataset.

| Component | Strategy |
|-----------|----------|
| **Code** | Open source (MIT License) — community can contribute, fork, and learn |
| **Deployment** | Centralized on soil.rip — single canonical instance |
| **Data** | Proprietary — the irreplaceable moat |
| **AI Models** | Proprietary — trained on unique dataset |
| **Brand** | Trademarked — "SOIL" protected |

### 1.2 Key Benefits

- **Scalability:** Community handles localization, features, and regional adaptation
- **Trust:** Open methodology builds academic and research credibility
- **Succession:** Platform survives even if SOIL as entity fails
- **Talent:** Contributors become candidates for Keeper and staff roles
- **Defensibility:** Data moat cannot be replicated by forking code

## 2. Open Source Architecture

### 2.1 Repository Structure

SOIL's codebase is organized into focused repositories, each serving a specific function:

| Repository | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| `soil-cenotaphery` | Unity 3D viewer — immersive cenotaphery environment |

### 2.2 What Remains Proprietary

The following components are never open-sourced:

1. Production database and all cenotaph data
2. Trained LLM weights and AI models
3. Verification credentials and API keys
4. Research datasets (anonymized or raw)
5. Production infrastructure configurations

## 3. Keeper Economics

Keepers are local operators who drive SOIL's global expansion. They are not just moderators — they are franchise partners with aligned economic incentives.

### 3.1 Keeper Hierarchy

| Level | Responsibility | Example |
|-------|----------------|---------|
| **Country Keeper** | Entire country (early stage) | Keeper of Nigeria |
| **Regional Keeper** | State/province (after country grows) | Keeper of Lagos State |
| **City Keeper** | Metropolitan area (after region grows) | Keeper of Lagos |

Keepers advance when their zone reaches capacity (1,024 cenotaphs) and splits. They choose to remain as coordinator or become hands-on regional operator.

### 3.2 Revenue Streams

#### Transaction Share

Keepers earn percentage of all purchases within their region:

| Transaction Type | Keeper Share |
|------------------|--------------|
| Cenotaph redesign | 25% |
| Premium features | 20-30% |
| Crypt storage upgrades | 20% |

#### Pathologist Fees

Keepers can conduct founder interviews themselves:

- Fee per interview: $50-150 (varies by region)
- Keeper receives: 80% of fee
- SOIL receives: 20% of fee
- Language advantage: Local Keepers conduct interviews in native language

#### Event Revenue

Keepers organize local events with revenue retention:

- Sponsorship revenue: 70% to Keeper
- Ticket sales (if applicable): 80% to Keeper
- Required: SOIL branding at all events

#### Additional Revenue

1. Referral bonuses: $5-10 or 50 Respects per verified cenotaph from referral
2. Consultation marketplace: 5-10% of consultation fees in their region

### 3.3 Example Monthly Income

**Lagos Keeper, Year 2 (200 cenotaphs, ~500 visits/day):**

| Revenue Source | Amount |
|----------------|--------|
| Transaction share (redesigns, premium) | $52.50 |
| Pathologist interviews (4 × $80 × 80%) | $256.00 |
| Monthly meetup sponsorship (2 × $100 × 70%) | $140.00 |
| Referrals (8 × $7) | $56.00 |
| **TOTAL MONTHLY** | **≈ $500** |

*Note: In emerging markets like Nigeria, this represents significant additional income. As cenotaphery grows, income scales proportionally.*

### 3.4 Keeper Responsibilities

| Responsibility | Frequency | Performance Metric |
|----------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Moderate new cenotaphs | Daily | < 48h review time |
| Verification spot checks | Weekly | Fraud rate < 2% |
| Community engagement | Ongoing | < 24h response time |
| Organize meetups | Quarterly minimum | Min 1 per quarter |
| Localization feedback | Ongoing | Active in channels |
| Monthly reporting | Monthly | Dashboard submission |

### 3.5 Keeper Onboarding Path

**Stage 1: Applicant**

1. Submits application with local startup ecosystem credentials
2. Demonstrates connection to founder community
3. Passes basic background verification

**Stage 2: Apprentice (3 months)**

1. Works under existing Keeper or SOIL team supervision
2. Learns moderation, verification, and community management
3. Receives 50% of normal revenue share during apprenticeship
4. May be declined if performance insufficient

**Stage 3: Full Keeper**

1. Full administrative rights and revenue share
2. Annual confirmation through community vote
3. Can train and supervise new Apprentices

**Stage 4: Senior Keeper (Optional)**

1. Coordinates multiple Keepers at country level
2. Advisory role in regional strategy
3. Bonus share from coordinated regions

## 4. Contributor Workflow

Founders are among the most entrepreneurial and creative people in the world. SOIL harnesses this creativity through a structured contribution system that benefits both the platform and contributors.

### 4.1 Contribution Types

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Code** | Bug fixes and performance improvements, new features and UI enhancements, localization (i18n) and accessibility |
| **Design** | UI/UX improvements, cenotaph visual styles and themes, regional design customizations, marketing and brand assets |
| **Content** | Documentation and tutorials, translations to new languages, case studies and research |
| **Research** | Framework analysis, data insights and patterns, methodology review |

### 4.2 Contributor Journey

**Stage 1: Discovery**

Contributor finds SOIL GitHub, reads CONTRIBUTING.md, explores "good first issues" labeled for newcomers.

**Stage 2: First Contribution**

Forks repository, makes small fix, opens PR, receives code review from maintainers. After merge, appears in Contributors list.

**Stage 3: Regular Contributor**

Multiple accepted PRs earn "Contributor" badge on platform profile. Access to contributor Discord/Slack, can take on complex issues.

**Stage 4: Core Contributor**

Significant contributions grant code review rights, influence on roadmap discussions, invitation to contributor calls.

**Stage 5: Maintainer**

Rare distinction with merge rights, architectural decision authority, potential path to core team or paid roles.

### 4.3 Recognition System

#### Public Recognition

- Name in CONTRIBUTORS.md file
- Contributor badge on SOIL profile
- Special flair on personal cenotaph (if applicable)
- Annual "Top Contributors" spotlight

#### Respects Rewards

| Contribution | Respects Earned |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Merged PR (small — bug fix, typo) | 50 Respects |
| Merged PR (medium — feature, improvement) | 150 Respects |
| Merged PR (large — significant feature) | 500 Respects |
| Accepted design contribution | 200 Respects |
| Complete language translation | 1,000 Respects |
| Documentation page | 100 Respects |

#### Tangible Benefits

- SOIL swag (stickers, shirts, limited merchandise)
- Conference tickets when SOIL sponsors events
- Reference letters for job applications
- Priority consideration for Keeper, Pathologist, and staff positions

### 4.4 Idea → Implementation Flow

Community-driven feature development follows a structured process:

**Step 1: Idea Submission**

Contributors submit ideas via GitHub Discussions or dedicated Canny board using template: Problem, Proposed Solution, Expected Impact. Community comments and refines proposals.

**Step 2: Community Voting**

Upvotes determine interest level. Discussion continues for minimum 2 weeks to refine scope and gather feedback from diverse perspectives.

**Step 3: Triage**

SOIL team reviews top-voted ideas and applies labels: "approved" (ready for work), "needs-design" (requires UX work first), "future" (good idea, not now), "wontfix" (declined with reasoning). Approved ideas become GitHub Issues.

**Step 4: Claiming**

Contributors comment "I'll take this" to claim issues. First qualified claim wins with 2-week deadline (extendable). High-priority unclaimed issues may receive bounty.

**Step 5: Implementation**

Contributor works in fork, opens Draft PR early for feedback. Final PR includes tests and documentation updates.

**Step 6: Review & Deploy**

Code review by 1-2 reviewers, design review if UI changes. Merge to main triggers auto-deploy to staging. Production deployment in next release cycle.

**Step 7: Credit**

Contributor credited in release notes, Respects awarded. Original idea author also receives Respects for contribution to platform improvement.

### 4.5 Bounty System

For priority work, SOIL offers financial bounties funded by operational budget or feature sponsors:

| Tier | Amount | Scope |
|------|--------|-------|
| 🟢 Small | $50–100 | Bug fixes, small improvements (< 4 hours estimated) |
| 🟡 Medium | $100–300 | New features, significant fixes (4–20 hours estimated) |
| 🔴 Large | $300–1,000 | Major features, complex systems (20+ hours estimated) |
| ⭐ Strategic | Negotiable | Critical infrastructure, specialized expertise required |

## 5. Monetization Strategy

Open source code enables adoption; data moat enables monetization. The code is the distribution channel, not the product.

### 5.1 Why Open Source Doesn't Undermine Revenue

Founders seeking closure want THE community, not "a" community. Forking code produces an empty cemetery with no data, no network effects, no verification system, and no brand trust. The value is in the ecosystem, not the code.

### 5.2 B2C Revenue (Founders)

| Revenue Stream | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| Premium cenotaph features | Redesign, expanded Crypt storage, priority placement |
| Pathologist interviews | Human-conducted therapeutic interviews (vs. AI) |
| Consultation visibility | Boost in founder marketplace for consulting |
| Anti-aging & customization | Respects purchases for cenotaph beautification |

### 5.3 B2B Revenue (Institutions)

| Revenue Stream | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| Research API access | Aggregated patterns, benchmarks, mortality data |
| Custom analytics | "Show mortality in fintech Series A in Europe" |
| Predictive models | Trained AI for portfolio risk assessment |
| White-label diagnostics | Integrate SOIL health scores into partner tools |
| Early access | New research before public release |

### 5.4 Services Revenue

| Service | Why Not Forkable |
|---------|------------------|
| Verification network | Requires Keeper network, reputation system |
| AI extraction | Requires models trained on proprietary data |
| Research reports | Requires dataset + research team |
| Founder matching | Requires critical mass on both sides |
| Certification programs | Requires brand authority |

### 5.5 Revenue Timeline

**Year 1–2 (Stealth Phase)**

- Selective B2B under NDA
- Patreon and community donations
- Premium Pathologist interviews

**Year 2–3 (Scale Phase)**

- Tiered API access for institutions
- Premium founder features
- Accelerator partnership deals

**Year 3+ (Ecosystem Phase)**

- Diagnostics Center subscriptions
- Clinic marketplace fees
- Educational Institute tuition
- Certification programs

## 6. Ecosystem Synergy

The development ecosystem creates a self-reinforcing cycle that grows organically:

### 6.1 The Virtuous Cycle

> *Founder creates cenotaph → Sees improvement opportunity → Submits idea → Implements feature → Earns Respects + recognition → Becomes regular contributor → Qualifies as Keeper → Grows local ecosystem → Attracts more founders → More cenotaphs → More ideas → Cycle continues*

### 6.2 Stakeholder Alignment

| Stakeholder | Aligned Incentives |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Founders** | Closure and dignity for their venture; Community connection and support; Platform improvements benefit their cenotaph; Path to Keeper role and income |
| **Contributors** | Recognition and portfolio building; Respects with real value; Career advancement opportunities; Impact on meaningful mission |
| **Keepers** | Sustainable income from growing region; Community leadership status; Direct influence on platform direction; Entrepreneurial opportunity |
| **SOIL** | Global scaling without central overhead; Continuous platform improvement; Defensible data moat; Mission achievement |

### 6.3 Network Effects

Multiple reinforcing network effects compound SOIL's defensibility:

**Data Network Effect**

More cenotaphs → richer dataset → better insights → higher value for all users → attracts more founders

**Community Network Effect**

More founders → better matching → more consultations → more testimonials → attracts more founders

**Development Network Effect**

More contributors → better platform → more users → more contributors

**Geographic Network Effect**

More Keepers → more regions covered → more local founders reached → more cenotaphs → more Keepers needed

## 7. Implementation Roadmap

### 7.1 Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1–6)

- Set up GitHub organization and repository structure
- Create CONTRIBUTING.md, CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md, and contributor documentation
- Establish issue templates and labeling system
- Launch contributor Discord/Slack channel
- Onboard first 5–10 pilot Keepers in strategic regions
- Build basic contributor recognition system

### 7.2 Phase 2: Growth (Months 7–12)

- Scale Keeper network to 20+ regions
- Launch idea voting platform (GitHub Discussions or Canny)
- Implement Respects rewards for contributors
- First bounty program for priority features
- Establish Keeper economics tracking dashboard
- First Day of the Dead Venture celebration (global)

### 7.3 Phase 3: Scale (Months 13–24)

- Expand to 50+ Keeper regions globally
- Formal Keeper apprenticeship program launch
- Core Contributor program with maintainer track
- Community-driven localization to 10+ languages
- Keeper governance and election system
- Senior Keeper tier for country-level coordination

### 7.4 Phase 4: Maturity (Year 2+)

- 100+ Keeper regions with self-sustaining economics
- DAO elements for community governance
- Contributor-to-staff pipeline established
- Full localization in 25+ languages
- Self-sustaining contribution ecosystem

## 9. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Fork creates competitor | Data moat is unforkable; brand protection; community loyalty; network effects |
| Keeper quality issues | Apprenticeship program; performance metrics; community voting; removal mechanism |
| Contributor burnout | Recognition system; Respects rewards; clear boundaries; bounties for priority work |
| Regional economic imbalance | Localized pricing; grant support for emerging markets; adjusted revenue shares |
| Code quality degradation | Code review requirements; testing standards; maintainer oversight; CI/CD gates |
| Governance disputes | Clear documentation; escalation paths; community voting; founder authority in Phase 1–2 |

## 10. Conclusion

SOIL's development ecosystem strategy positions the platform for sustainable global growth while maintaining the integrity of its core mission. By opening the code while centralizing data and deployment, SOIL gains:

- **Global scaling** through Keepers who operate as franchise partners with aligned incentives
- **Continuous improvement** through community-driven development
- **Unassailable data moat** that compounds in value over time
- **Research credibility** through transparent methodology
- **Succession insurance** ensuring the mission survives any organizational challenges

The virtuous cycle — where founders become contributors, contributors become Keepers, and Keepers build local ecosystems that attract more founders — creates a self-reinforcing growth engine that scales organically while serving SOIL's founding mission:

> ***Transform organizational failure from wasted potential into collective wisdom.***
